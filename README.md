# Article

**[How an AirTag Planted by a Reporter Led to a Secret Amazon Site Where Old Books Are Cut Apart and Scanned](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/how-an-airtag-planted-by-a-reporter-led-to-a-secret-amazon-site-where-old-books-are-cut-apart-and-scanned/)**
*GeekWire — 2026*

This article discusses a journalist's investigation into **Amazon's mass purchasing of books**. The journalist placed an AirTag inside a rare book and tracked it until it reached an Amazon warehouse. The investigation revealed that companies are purchasing books to obtain material for **training AI models**, including older books that were never digitized before the AI boom. Some books have their spines removed and pages scanned, effectively destroying the physical copies while converting their contents into digital training data. Rare books have become particularly valuable because they may contain information that is not already available in AI datasets, leading companies to pay large amounts of money to acquire them.

I found this interesting because of the **extreme lengths journalists have to go to uncover what companies are doing** and because of the secrecy surrounding how AI training data is obtained. I'm not an avid reader, but I think there is something **dystopian about destroying physical books to train AI models**, especially when some of them are rare or original copies that may never exist in their original form again. The legal side is also interesting, as the use of legally acquired books for AI training has been the subject of court cases and ongoing debate over **fair use**. Overall, the article made me think about the tradeoff between preserving physical knowledge and extracting that knowledge to build increasingly powerful AI systems.
